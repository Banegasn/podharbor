01 / INSPECT

Find the pod behind it.

Browse pods, Deployments, and StatefulSets. Check restarts, replica health, container states, and image versions. Open the YAML when you need the details.

02 / FOLLOW

Put the logs together.

Follow several pods across namespaces in one timeline. Filter by text or regex, spot errors and warnings, and export the logs you need.

03 / STAY ORIENTED

Keep your context.

Switch between your existing kubeconfig contexts. Select the namespaces you work in, including manually added namespaces when cluster-wide listing is restricted.

Already using kubectl?

You're already set up.

PodHarbor reads $KUBECONFIG or ~/.kube/config and uses your existing authentication plugins. Keep your credentials on your machine and work within your Kubernetes permissions.

Pod deletion and rollout restarts ask for confirmation. No in-cluster agent to install.
